## The case in five sourced facts

1. MTD for Income Tax is live since 6 April 2026 for >£50k sole traders and landlords — ~780,000 people now, ~970,000 more in April 2027 — and the **first-ever quarterly update is due 7 August 2026** (gov.uk).
2. Agents file **59% of the UK's 11.2m self assessment returns** — the accountant is the buyer, and one practice brings dozens of mandated clients (HMRC via AccountingWEB).
3. **59% of accountants say at least half their income-tax clients still aren't using digital tools** as MTD goes live (Wolters Kluwer, Apr 2026).
4. The profession's trade press — **published 10 July 2026, the day before this run** — opens with the exact scene: *"your client has just sent a blurry photo of a petrol receipt through WhatsApp"*, and advises surcharging clients with "WhatsApp habits" at premium fees. The habit is treated as a cost; nobody has made it the channel.
5. Every component already exists separately and is validated — Dext's WhatsApp letterbox, ClientWindow's WhatsApp↔office pipe, Snyp/Receiptor's scanners, practice-management reminder emails — but **no player holds the intersection**: an obligation-aware agent that extracts, chases gaps, escalates, and hands the practice a review-ready quarter.

## Top risk, stated plainly

No structural moat on day 1. Dext could bolt chasing onto its WhatsApp channel; Xero went "all in on AI" at Xerocon London three days ago. The defence is speed through live quarterly cycles and practice-workflow depth — and the kill criterion is explicit: if an incumbent ships a credible clerk before practice #5 signs, pivot (risk B1). Second honest caveat: quarterly-update penalties are waived for 2026-27, so the sale must run on practice margin, not client fear (risk B2).

## Verification pass (R3-D12)

The HMRC policy paper was fetched directly at run end. Confirmed verbatim: dates, thresholds (incl. the 2028 £20k tier), the 780,000/970,000 populations, and "interim cumulative reports". The pass also *strengthened* the case — HMRC's own estimate of ~£110/yr continuing compliance cost per mandated business is now the plan's pricing sanity check (M13). One caveat stands: the 2026-27 quarterly-penalty grace rests on two secondary sources (LITRG fetch failed); flagged in the evidence log and, being an anti-urgency fact, errs against us rather than for us.
